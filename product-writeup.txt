As AI makes products easier to build, more businesses will compete for customer attention. Small teams can develop useful products but often lack the skills, time, or confidence to create compelling social media content. They need access to people who can communicate naturally, demonstrate a product, and produce videos worth publishing.

**The product is a marketplace for outsourcing social media content creation.** Businesses commission creators to produce content for their own accounts and advertising. Creators can be everyday people with strong presentation skills, experienced video producers, or influencers. Their portfolio and ability to deliver suitable content matter more than their follower count; publishing to their own audience is an optional service.

The central experience revolves around a **content request**. A business describes its product, intended audience, desired content, budget, and expectations. Creators review the opportunity and respond with their interest and relevant work, allowing the business to choose someone suitable. Businesses can also browse creator profiles and commission them directly. The platform supports the transaction from agreement through communication, delivery, revisions, payment, and feedback.

A representative customer is a small software team that wants a casual, selfie-style video explaining a relatable problem, demonstrating its app, and encouraging viewers to try it. The platform should help that team find someone who understands the product, speaks the audience’s language, and can deliver a finished video without requiring the team to become experts in video production.

**The product should make outsourcing feel simple and dependable.** Businesses need clear deliverables, credible examples of a creator’s abilities, and confidence that the work will arrive as agreed. Creators need clear expectations, suitable opportunities, reliable payment, and a way to build repeat business. Successful relationships should be easy to continue, with product context and previous feedback carrying forward into subsequent commissions.

The initial product should focus on a specific customer segment and a manageable set of content formats. Software demonstrations, potentially for Arabic-speaking audiences, are a promising starting hypothesis given the founding team’s experience. The platform should initially facilitate work between businesses and creators; assistance with briefs, concepts, editing, and recurring content production can expand as actual demand becomes clear. Revenue would initially come from a fee on completed commissions.

Airbnb-like browsing, a lightweight offer-and-acceptance flow, and playful branding such as “Yapp” are possible design directions, not requirements. The guiding principle is that discovering talent, agreeing on work, and receiving content should be straightforward.

Existing platforms already support content-only commissions, so the opportunity depends on execution and focus. **The goal is to become the easiest, most dependable place for a particular type of business to get social content made.** Early success should be judged by completed commissions, customer satisfaction, repeat purchases, and how little coordination is required to produce an acceptable result.
